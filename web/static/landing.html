Built to be explored, and to be questioned

The viewer shows one scan at a time, in 3D and on the original MRI, side by side.

3D and slices, linked

Click a point on the 3D model and the axial, coronal and sagittal slices jump to it. The tumor outline follows on every slice.

A guided tour

A short flythrough takes you from the raw sequences to the segmented tumor, its volume and its neighbours, with captions written from the case's own numbers.

Structure explorer

Search for a structure, isolate it, fade the rest, and see how far it is from the tumor. Keyboard shortcuts keep it quick.

An uncertainty view

Compare the model mask with an expert second mask when available, and read Dice scores for whole tumor, core and enhancing regions.

Trust badges

Registration quality, a size check against known tumors and an "unverified" label stay on screen. When the atlas does not line up, the location is withheld.

A one-page report

Download the volumes, the key views and the caveats on a single page you can share.

What this is not

Not a diagnosis. It is a research prototype and must not be used to make medical decisions.

Tested on BraTS, not on patients. Accuracy is measured on public BraTS cases that have expert outlines. On real scans without expert outlines, every result is a model estimate and is marked unverified.

Location can be withheld. A poor atlas match, a tiny volume or a fragmented tumor all stop the viewer from naming a lobe.
